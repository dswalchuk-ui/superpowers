# 2. Fulfillment Setup

How to sell and ship your existing hard copies from home **cleanly**, and how
print-on-demand plugs in later **without reworking the store.**

*Canada Post details confirmed July 2026; sources at bottom. Rates change — always
price the actual parcel in Snap Ship before quoting a shipping charge.*

---

## Stage 1 — self-ship your existing inventory (now)

You have books already made and paid for. The goal is a repeatable
order→pack→label→tracking loop that takes minutes, not a logistics operation.

### The key Canada fact
**There is no Canadian equivalent of US "media mail."** Books ship as:
- **Lettermail / oversize Lettermail** — only for items up to **500 g** and within
  size limits. A single thin softcover *might* qualify; most how-to books with
  450+ photos will not.
- **Regular Parcel** (cheapest parcel, slower) or **Expedited Parcel** — this is
  what most book orders will use. Price by weight + zone (local/regional/national).

Weigh one of each title on a kitchen scale once you have them; that number drives
everything. A typical trade-size how-to paperback lands ~400–700 g; hardcovers
more.

### Packaging (cheap, protects books)
- **Rigid/stay-flat mailers** for single softcovers — prevents corner/bend damage.
  Buy in bulk (Uline Canada, Amazon.ca, or a local packaging supplier).
- **Bubble mailers** for lighter single books.
- **Small corrugated boxes** for 2+ books or hardcovers, with a bit of paper fill.
- Buy one pack of each, don't over-order until you see what actually sells.

### Where to buy postage + print labels
- **Canada Post [Snap Ship](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/small-business/snap-ship.page)** — free tool: compare services,
  get **discounted small-business rates**, print labels, book pickups. This is your
  default for Stage 1.
- **Sign up for [Solutions for Small Business](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/small-business.page)** (free) — gives you the
  discounted commercial rates instead of retail counter rates. Do this before your
  first shipment; it pays for itself immediately.
- **Tracking** is included on Regular/Expedited Parcel — the tracking number is on
  the label; email it to the customer (see workflow below).

### The clean weekly shipping workflow
Batch it. Don't ship one-off every time an order dings.

1. **Orders accumulate** in Payhip (you get an email per sale).
2. **Once or twice a week** (e.g. Wednesday + Saturday), sit down with the pile:
3. For each order: pack the book → weigh it → create the label in Snap Ship →
   pay + print.
4. **Copy the tracking number** into Payhip's order (or just email the customer:
   "Shipped! Track it here: [link]"). Mark the order fulfilled.
5. Drop the batch at the post office / mailbox, or book a **Snap Ship pickup**.

Keep a **shipping supplies bin** by the door: mailers, tape, a printed label sheet,
the scale. Packing 5 orders at once takes ~20 minutes. Packing them one at a time
across the week takes an hour of context-switching. Batch.

### Setting the shipping charge in the store
- Weigh each title once, price a national Regular Parcel in Snap Ship, and set a
  **flat shipping rate per book in Canada** that covers your average (e.g. a flat
  $X for the first book, less for each additional).
- **Optional:** offer "free shipping" and bake the cost into the book price — many
  buyers prefer one number. Your call; flat-rate is simpler to start.
- **Start Canada-only.** Add US shipping later once the Canadian flow is smooth —
  US customs forms add friction you don't need in week one.

## Stage 2 — print-on-demand takes over (later, when stock runs low)

The whole point: **when you migrate to Shopify and your self-shipped stock runs
low, POD flips on at the product level with no store rebuild.**

### How it plugs in
On Shopify (your "later" platform — see doc 1), install the **Lulu Direct** app (or
Gelato):
1. Upload each book's **print-ready interior PDF + cover PDF** to Lulu once.
2. Link the Lulu print product to your existing store listing.
3. From then on, an order on your store → Lulu auto-prints and ships it → you keep
   the margin. **You touch nothing.**

### The switchover trigger (keep it dead simple)
> **When a title drops to ~5 copies on hand, enable its Lulu POD link and stop
> selling the self-ship version.**

Set a reminder at that threshold. No overlap, no double-selling, no warehouse.

### Parallel channel: Amazon KDP
Separately, list titles on **Amazon KDP** for reach. That's Amazon fulfilling
Amazon orders — independent of your store. Different list price per channel so each
stays profitable (Lulu direct nets you more per book; Amazon gets you discovery).

### What you need ready for POD (gather now, from Stan)
- Print-ready **interior PDF** per title (correct trim size, bleed, embedded fonts).
- Print-ready **cover PDF** (spine width depends on page count + paper — Lulu's
  cover template generates this).
- The **ISBN** per title (or use Lulu's free ISBN for direct-only sales; you need
  your own to sell on Amazon/expanded distribution).

If Stan only has printed books and no digital source files, that's the one real
blocker — flag it early. See doc 3 for the "no source files" path (scanning/
re-typesetting), which applies to POD too.

## Concrete next steps
1. **This week:** sign up for Canada Post Solutions for Small Business (free).
2. **When books arrive:** weigh one of each title, write the weights down here.
3. **Before first sale:** buy one pack of stay-flat mailers + tape; set the flat
   Canada shipping rate in Payhip.
4. **Defer:** everything Lulu/KDP until stock runs low or you migrate to Shopify.
