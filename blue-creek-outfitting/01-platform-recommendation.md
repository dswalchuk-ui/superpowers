# 1. Platform Recommendation

**The question:** what to build a fresh store + website on, given it must sell
physical books (self-shipped now, print-on-demand later) AND digital products
(ebooks, video) — run by one person with a full-time job, in Canada.

*Pricing below confirmed July 2026; sources at the bottom. Platform pricing shifts,
so re-check before you commit money.*

---

## The recommendation: Payhip now → Shopify later

**Start on [Payhip](https://payhip.com) free tier. Migrate to [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com) Basic once you're consistently selling.**

This is a phased pick, not a fence-sit, and it's chosen specifically for *your*
situation (Section 9 of the brief):

- Paying **~$51 CAD/month for Shopify before your first sale** is the exact kind of
  upfront commitment that makes month one feel like a loss and triggers the quit
  pattern. Payhip is **$0/month**, so the only cost is a 5% fee *when you actually
  sell something.* No sale, no cost. That matches "test small, then scale."
- Payhip handles **everything you need for the "now" stage**: digital products
  (ebooks, video/streaming) with instant delivery, AND physical products you ship
  yourself. One platform covers the whole first phase.
- The natural graduation point is real: when you're selling consistently and want a
  fuller branded website + automated print-on-demand + smoother shipping labels,
  **that's** when Shopify earns its monthly fee. By then you'll have revenue to pay
  for it and validated demand to justify it.
- **The migration is small.** You'll have a handful of titles, not hundreds.
  Re-listing them on Shopify later is an evening's work, not a rebuild — and it only
  happens after the business has proven itself.

## Why not the others as the *starting* point

- **Shopify** is the right *destination*, not the right *start*. It's the most
  hands-off full platform and the best for physical + POD, but the monthly fee
  before validation fights the whole "small, finishable, low-risk" premise. Move
  here when sales are steady.
- **WooCommerce** is cheapest at scale and infinitely flexible, but it's
  self-hosted WordPress: **you** own the updates, security patches, backups, and the
  inevitable "the site broke on a Sunday" evenings. For a time-poor solo operator
  whose scarce resource is hours, this is the wrong trade. Skip it.

## The print-on-demand nuance (important — don't skip)

"Print-on-demand" is not one thing, and getting this wrong causes rework later:

- **Amazon KDP** is its own storefront/**channel** — your book listed for sale *on
  Amazon*, printed and shipped by Amazon when someone buys it there. It does **not**
  fulfill orders placed on your own store. Great for reach; it's a parallel channel,
  not your store's fulfillment.
- **Store-integrated POD** — a book someone buys *on your store* that gets
  auto-printed and shipped — is **Lulu (Lulu Direct app)** or **Gelato**. These plug
  into Shopify/WooCommerce at the product level.

**Recommended POD strategy (for the "later" stage):**
1. List physical books on **Amazon KDP** as a parallel channel for discovery/reach.
2. On your own store, use **Lulu Direct** for store-integrated POD once your
   self-shipped stock runs low.

Because POD only matters *after* your existing hard-copy stock is gone (well down
the road), this is a Phase 2/3 concern. It's a strong reason your *destination* is
Shopify — but it does not change the recommendation to *start* on Payhip.

Cost/quality reality check (per the research): Lulu prints cost more per unit than
KDP (e.g. ~$7 vs ~$3.66 on one test book), but because you keep ~80% selling direct
vs KDP taking ~30–40%, **you often net more per book selling direct via Lulu.** Set
a different list price per channel so each stays profitable.

## Concrete next step (do this in one evening)

1. Go to [payhip.com](https://payhip.com) → create the **Free Forever** account.
2. Connect **Stripe** and/or **PayPal** (this is how you get paid; both are free to
   connect, Canadian bank supported).
3. Create **one draft product** — the Trail Riding & Packing book — with a
   placeholder price and cover, just to learn the dashboard. Don't publish.
4. Stop there. Publishing for real waits on the inventory count + files from Stan
   (Track A in the README). You've now done the platform piece.
